# Methodology — Capital Region vs. Non-Capital Region Competition and 90-Day Follow-Up Supply

## Research question

Did the large gap in first-priority apartment subscription competition between the capital region and non-capital region translate into a similarly large gap in observed follow-up supply within 90 days?

## Source

Underlying project-level file:

`reports/apttosell-apartment-subscription-followup-projects-2026-jan-sep.csv`

Source data originate from Korea Real Estate Board ApplyHome public data and AptToSell project-level matching.

## Observation cutoff

2026-10-04.

## Fixed cohort

The analysis uses only projects that:

1. have a valid first-priority competition rate; and
2. have at least 90 days of observation between the initial recruitment announcement and the observation cutoff.

Fixed cohort: **140 projects**.

- Capital region: 69
- Non-capital region: 71

## Region definition

Capital region = Seoul, Incheon, Gyeonggi.

Non-capital region = all other regions.

## Metrics

For each region:

- arithmetic mean first-priority competition rate;
- median first-priority competition rate;
- share of projects below 1:1;
- share of projects at or above 10:1;
- share with a first observed follow-up supply event within 90 days.

## Results

Capital region:

- median competition rate: 3.92:1
- mean competition rate: 46.53:1
- below 1:1: 21 of 69 (30.4%)
- at or above 10:1: 26 of 69 (37.7%)
- follow-up within 90 days: 40 of 69 (58.0%)

Non-capital region:

- median competition rate: 0.49:1
- mean competition rate: 4.50:1
- below 1:1: 44 of 71 (62.0%)
- at or above 10:1: 7 of 71 (9.9%)
- follow-up within 90 days: 43 of 71 (60.6%)

## Interpretation

The initial subscription-demand indicators show a large regional divide, but the observed 90-day follow-up-supply rate differs by only 2.6 percentage points.

This does **not** mean that capital and non-capital markets are equally healthy.

Follow-up supply is not the same as:

- unsold-housing rate;
- contract-failure rate;
- cancellation rate;
- sales-completion rate.

The result is descriptive and does not establish causality.

The mean competition rate is heavily affected by a small number of very high-competition projects, so the median and threshold shares should be emphasized in reporting.
